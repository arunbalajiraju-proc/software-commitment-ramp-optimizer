# Organizational pilot playbook

## Purpose

Use this playbook to test the tool on one live sourcing event or renewal before any
enterprise integration work. The pilot should determine whether the workflow improves
the decision record and commercial outcome—not merely whether the Streamlit page runs.

## Best first use cases

Choose an agreement with:

- a named licence metric such as users, devices, cores, or sites;
- a material difference between day-one need and steady-state demand;
- deployment, migration, security, integration, or change dependencies;
- a supplier willing to quote more than one commitment structure; and
- buyer-accessible usage data after award.

Good examples include an M365 add-on rollout, ERP cloud migration, CRM expansion,
cybersecurity platform deployment, employee-experience platform, or enterprise AI-user
licence rollout.

Do not use the current engine without adaptation for purely consumption-priced services,
token commitments, unlimited enterprise licences, outcomes-based fees, or heterogeneous
SKU bundles whose economics cannot be represented by one consistent unit.

## Pilot team

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Procurement lead | Owns the workflow, supplier pricing schedule, and negotiation record |
| Business/application owner | Validates eligible population and expected adoption |
| Project manager | Provides dated deployment waves and dependency status |
| Architecture/IT lead | Confirms capacity and technical readiness |
| Security/privacy lead | Confirms critical approvals and conditions |
| Finance | Reviews budget, P90, and accounting implications |
| SAM/ITAM or usage owner | Defines active use and performs recurring reconciliation |
| Legal | Converts the selected commercial mechanics into enforceable language |

## Four-week pilot

### Week 1: Define and validate

1. Select one live deal and define exactly what one licence unit represents.
2. Reconcile steady-state and day-one populations to named source records.
3. Complete the readiness gate with evidence owners in one working session.
4. Record blockers rather than changing status to make the result pass.

### Week 2: Source comparable structures

1. Download the supplier-pricing request.
2. Require a full commitment, recommended phased activation, and phased true-down offer.
3. Specify activation, measurement, notice, floor, billing, overage, and repricing rules.
4. Reject commercial rows that cannot be normalized to the same licence metric.

### Week 3: Evaluate and negotiate

1. Enter compliant offers in the comparison tab.
2. Review expected cost, P90, unused exposure, utilization, and break-even premium.
3. Test sensitivities for rollout confidence, completion timing, and final demand.
4. Record qualitative value and constraints that the numerical model does not capture.
5. Agree the initial PO, later activation gates, and review owner.

### Week 4: Operationalize

1. Attach the approval plan and model record to the procurement file.
2. Put the usage report, review cadence, adjustment formula, and remedies in the contract.
3. Schedule monthly reconciliations and every formal quantity-review date.
4. Run a sample usage review using masked or test data.
5. Document lessons and decide whether to expand, modify, or stop the pilot.

## Pilot success measures

The pilot is successful when it produces evidence of control improvement, including:

- every material quantity has a source and accountable owner;
- known readiness gaps produce a hold or documented exception;
- at least two comparable commitment structures are obtained;
- the evaluation includes P90 and unused exposure, not unit price alone;
- the selected contract contains measurable activation and adjustment mechanics;
- a named owner completes the first usage reconciliation on time; and
- the final decision can be reproduced from retained inputs and supplier terms.

Do not use “modelled difference” as the primary pilot KPI. Track realized commercial
changes separately, such as deferred activation, reduced initial PO, negotiated true-down,
credits, price protection, or avoided net-new units. Finance should validate any reported
savings or cost avoidance under the organization's policy.

## Data and deployment

For a pilot involving confidential quotes or usage data:

1. clone the repository into the organization's approved environment;
2. restrict access using existing identity and network controls;
3. avoid user-level personal data when aggregated counts are sufficient;
4. retain downloads in the approved procurement or contract repository; and
5. complete security, privacy, records-retention, and legal review before wider use.

The public demo is for learning, masked scenarios, and public evidence—not confidential
commercial evaluation.
